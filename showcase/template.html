What the learning algorithm does with this

This replay only shows rewards. During training, PPO divides rewards by a running standard deviation of the discounted return, so a −500 penalty becomes about −1 and learning stays stable. It then forms an advantage A = r + γV(s′) − V(s) (smoothed by GAE, γ 0.99, λ 0.95) and makes actions with positive advantage more likely. Without the rescaling the large penalties swamp learning.

How the two sides interact
PhishingLands on workstations. Chance = vector base × host vulnerability × zone pressure.
MalwareHits most hosts; fastest against unpatched IoT and workstations.
DDoSTargets servers and cloud; redirect_traffic cuts its landing chance ×0.1.
Escalation and theftEARLY → ESCALATED → BREACHED. Faster on weakly patched hosts, databases and cloud.
Lateral movementAn escalated host raises intrusion odds in the next zone (DMZ → Internal → Data tier).
block_ipReverts an early intrusion (70%); landing chance ×0.25 for 5 steps.
isolate_machineFreezes a host for 5 steps. −200 downtime.
patch_systemVulnerability ×0.6 and landing chance halved.
redirect_trafficScrubs traffic for 4 steps; strong against DDoS.
deploy_honeypot20-step decoy (max 3): 80% of landings on it are trapped, +50.
restore_backupCleans a host. −50 downtime. The only exit from breached.
What this demo does and does not show

The defender sees only noisy anomaly scores, smoothed over time, plus its own flags. The true stage (node colour) is hidden from it, which is why it sometimes acts on a false alarm or misses an intrusion. This is a custom simulator, not CybORG or CAGE; its numbers are not comparable to those benchmarks and make no claim about real-world defense. Defenders: PPO (single agent), MAPPO (three zone agents, three actions per step) and a rule-based heuristic.
